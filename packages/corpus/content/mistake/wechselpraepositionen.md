Nine prepositions — *an, auf, hinter, in, neben, über, unter, vor, zwischen* —
take either the accusative or the dative, and the case carries the meaning
rather than decorating it. Accusative for movement towards a place, dative for
a position.

*Ich gehe in **die** Stadt.* — I am going into town. Movement, accusative.
*Ich bin in **der** Stadt.* — I am in town. Position, dative.

---

The mistake that survives longest is choosing the case from the verb rather
than from the meaning. *Stellen*, *legen*, *setzen* and *hängen* describe putting
something somewhere and take the accusative; *stehen*, *liegen*, *sitzen* and
*hängen* describe where it already is and take the dative.

*Ich hänge das Bild an **die** Wand.* — I hang the picture onto the wall.
*Das Bild hängt an **der** Wand.* — The picture hangs on the wall.

---

The other reliable trap is that "movement" means movement *into the place named
by the preposition*, not movement in general.

*Ich laufe in **dem** Park.* — I am running around inside the park. Dative,
because the running does not cross into the park.
*Ich laufe in **den** Park.* — I am running into the park. Accusative.

A learner who is running and therefore reaches for the accusative gets the
second sentence when they meant the first.
