Punctuality in Germany is treated as a form of respect rather than as an
administrative detail, and the tolerances are narrower than in many countries.
For an appointment — a doctor, an office, a viewing, a class — *pünktlich* means
arriving a few minutes before the stated time, not at it.

For a private dinner invitation the convention is different and often catches
learners out: arriving exactly on time is fine, arriving early is not. Five to
ten minutes after the stated time is normal and gives the host a moment.

---

If you are going to be late, the expectation is that you say so before you are
late, not when you arrive. A short message is enough:

*Ich verspäte mich um zehn Minuten. Entschuldigung!*

Arriving fifteen minutes late without warning is genuinely rude in a way that
arriving fifteen minutes late having sent a message at the outset is not. The
offence is the silence rather than the delay.

The academic quarter — *das akademische Viertel* — is the one systematic
exception. A university lecture listed at 10:00 **c.t.** (*cum tempore*) begins at
10:15. Listed **s.t.** (*sine tempore*), it begins at 10:00 exactly.
